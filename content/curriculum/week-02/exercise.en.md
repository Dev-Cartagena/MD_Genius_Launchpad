## Goal

Turn your idea into a precise problem statement and a ranked map of the assumptions that could sink it. This becomes the test plan for next week's customer interviews, so the aim is clarity about what you know and what you are guessing, not a polished pitch.

Estimated time: about 3 hours.

## Steps

1. Run at least three empathy sessions with people who have the problem. Observe them at work if you can, ask about the last time the problem happened and note quotes, workarounds, emotions and contradictions. Fill in an empathy map (says, does, thinks, feels) after each one.
2. Score up to three candidate problems on severity, frequency and willingness to pay from 1 to 5. Write one line of evidence beside each score and mark it "observed", "heard" or "guessed".
3. Write a job statement for your user, and a second one for your buyer if they are different people: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."
4. Write your problem statement as a point of view: "[User] needs a way to [need] because [insight]." Check it against the four tests in lesson 2. Write any number you rely on, such as time lost per visit, as an estimate with its source until you have measured it.
5. Write three how-might-we questions from your point of view.
6. List at least twelve assumptions behind your idea and tag each as desirability, viability or feasibility. Include at least two about data rights, failure modes or regulation from lesson 3.
7. Place every assumption on a two-by-two grid of importance against evidence. Circle the three that are most important and least supported.
8. For each of those three, write a cheap test you can run in the next two weeks and the result that would prove you wrong.
9. Add a short responsible-AI note: your main data sources and your right to use them, your most severe failure mode and the oversight level you will use for it, and the regulatory tier and sector rules you think apply.

## Deliverable

A two-page document, as a PDF or a shared document, with these sections: Empathy highlights, Problem scores, Job statements, Problem statement, How-might-we questions, Assumption map (a photo or image of the grid is fine), Top three riskiest assumptions and tests, Responsible-AI note.

## How it will be reviewed

- Grounding: the problem statement draws on real observations, and the empathy highlights include at least one contradiction or surprise.
- Precision: the user and need are specific, the need is an action and the statement does not contain a solution.
- Honesty: estimates are labeled as estimates, and evidence is marked as observed, heard or guessed.
- Prioritization: the three riskiest assumptions are truly important and weakly supported, not the easiest ones to test.
- Testability: each test is cheap, can run within two weeks and has a clear result that would prove the assumption wrong.
- Responsibility: data rights, the most severe failure mode and the oversight level are addressed concretely.
