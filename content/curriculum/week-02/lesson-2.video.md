# Video: Design thinking for founders
Duration: ~3:15 · Format: 16:9 1920×1080 · Presenter: Maya Ortiz — calm, mid-30s Latin American founder-turned-investor, navy blazer, neutral studio

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:20)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered. Lower third: "Week 2 · Design thinking for founders". A question mark in Mist #97B7C1 rotates slowly behind the title.
- Narration EN: With AI, you can prototype almost anything in a day. That makes a different mistake more likely: building a brilliant answer to a question nobody asked. Design thinking, as the Stanford d.school teaches it, helps you find the right question first.
- Narración ES: Con la IA puedes prototipar casi cualquier cosa en un día. Eso vuelve más probable otro error: construir una respuesta brillante a una pregunta que nadie hizo. El design thinking, tal como lo enseña la d.school de Stanford, te ayuda a encontrar primero la pregunta correcta.

## Scene 2 — The five modes (0:20–0:40)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Five circles in a loose loop on Navy #082B42: "Empathize", "Define", "Ideate", "Prototype", "Test". Arrows run forward and also loop back between them. The first three circles brighten to White Smoke; the last two stay Steel #3A607D with the label "Weeks 3–5".
- Narration EN: The d.school describes five modes: empathize, define, ideate, prototype and test. They are modes, not steps. You move back and forth between them as you learn. This week we focus on the first three. Prototyping and testing come back when you design experiments and your MVP.
- Narración ES: La d.school describe cinco modos: empatizar, definir, idear, prototipar y probar. Son modos, no pasos. Vas y vienes entre ellos a medida que aprendes. Esta semana nos enfocamos en los tres primeros. Prototipar y probar vuelven cuando diseñes experimentos y tu MVP.

## Scene 3 — Empathize (0:40–1:05)
- Type: broll
- On screen: Image with slow push-in. Overlay: a four-quadrant empathy map in thin Mist lines, "Says", "Does", "Thinks", "Feels", with short notes appearing in each.
- Image prompt (if broll): Photorealistic documentary photo of a young East Asian founder in casual clothes standing slightly behind a middle-aged Latino HVAC technician in a loud mechanical room, the founder taking handwritten notes in a small notebook while the technician points at a gauge on a large air handler, cool industrial light mixed with daylight from a high window, candid observation, no logos or readable text.
- Narration EN: Empathy here is a research practice. Observe people doing the real work. Engage them by asking about the last time something went wrong, and keep asking why. Immerse yourself by trying the job. Capture exact quotes, workarounds, emotions and, above all, contradictions, when what people say differs from what they do.
- Narración ES: Aquí la empatía es una práctica de investigación. Observa a las personas haciendo el trabajo real. Conversa con ellas y pregúntales por la última vez que algo salió mal, y sigue preguntando por qué. Sumérgete probando el trabajo en carne propia. Registra citas textuales, soluciones improvisadas, emociones y, sobre todo, contradicciones, cuando lo que la gente dice no coincide con lo que hace.

## Scene 4 — Define a point of view (1:05–1:30)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A sentence builds in three colored blocks: "[User]" in Mist, "needs a way to [verb]" in Steel, "because [insight]" in White Smoke. A weak example fades in and gets struck through: "Technicians need a better app." The strong example replaces it, line by line.
- Narration EN: In the define mode you write a point of view: a user, a need and an insight. The need is a verb, not a feature. The insight is the surprising thing you learned. "Technicians need a better app" is weak. "A newer technician working alone needs a way to confirm a diagnosis, because calling for help feels like failing" gives you something real to solve.
- Narración ES: En el modo definir escribes un punto de vista: un usuario, una necesidad y un hallazgo. La necesidad es un verbo, no una función. El hallazgo es lo sorprendente que aprendiste. "Los técnicos necesitan una mejor app" es débil. "Una técnica con poca experiencia que trabaja sola necesita una forma de confirmar un diagnóstico, porque pedir ayuda se siente como fracasar" te da algo real que resolver.

## Scene 5 — How might we (1:30–1:50)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Three words animate apart and get annotated: "How" with "a solution exists", "might" with "it may not work", "we" with "shared effort". Below, three sample questions slide in. A dial labeled "too broad · just right · too narrow" settles in the middle.
- Narration EN: Then ideate with how-might-we questions. How assumes a solution exists, might allows it to fail, and we makes it shared. Keep them in the middle: not "how might we fix field service", and not "how might we add a button". Generate many ideas, then choose with criteria you agreed on in advance.
- Narración ES: Después idea con preguntas "¿cómo podríamos…?". El cómo supone que hay una solución, el podríamos permite que falle y el plural la vuelve compartida. Mantenlas en el punto medio: ni "¿cómo podríamos arreglar el servicio de campo?" ni "¿cómo podríamos agregar un botón?". Genera muchas ideas y luego elige con criterios acordados de antemano.

## Scene 6 — Assumption mapping (1:50–2:25)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A two-by-two grid. X axis "Evidence: have it → guessing". Y axis "Importance: low → critical". Cards tagged "Desirability", "Viability", "Feasibility" drop onto the grid. The top-right quadrant glows softly in Mist with the label "Test first". Fieldnote's card "Paperwork is the biggest pain" lands there, followed by "Contractors will pay".
- Narration EN: Every idea rests on assumptions. David J. Bland, co-author of Testing Business Ideas, popularized assumption mapping. Write each belief as a statement that could be false, tag it as desirability, viability or feasibility, and place it by importance and evidence. Fieldnote's riskiest card surprised them: "paperwork is the biggest pain". Technicians say it, but their worst stories are about misdiagnosis. Close behind: contractors will pay. Both go straight into next week's interviews.
- Narración ES: Toda idea descansa en supuestos. David J. Bland, coautor de Testing Business Ideas, popularizó el mapa de supuestos. Escribe cada creencia como una afirmación que podría ser falsa, clasifícala como deseabilidad, viabilidad o factibilidad y ubícala según su importancia y su evidencia. La tarjeta más riesgosa de Fieldnote los sorprendió: "el papeleo es el mayor dolor". Los técnicos lo dicen, pero sus peores historias tratan de diagnósticos equivocados. Muy cerca está: los contratistas pagarán. Ambas pasan directo a las entrevistas de la próxima semana.

## Scene 7 — Common mistakes (2:25–3:05)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya, medium close-up. Four short lines stack at screen right in Steel #3A607D, each with a thin Mist #97B7C1 marker: "Surveys instead of observation", "A solution hidden in the problem statement", "Questions too broad or too narrow", "Testing the comfortable assumptions first".
- Narration EN: Four mistakes come up again and again. The first is treating empathy as a survey, instead of watching people do the real work. The second is writing a problem statement that already contains your solution, like "technicians need a voice app". The third is asking how-might-we questions that are so broad they produce nothing, or so narrow they lead to one feature. And the fourth is listing assumptions without ranking them, so you end up testing the comfortable ones first. Your map is a living document. Update it every week as evidence comes in.
- Narración ES: Hay cuatro errores que aparecen una y otra vez. El primero es tratar la empatía como una encuesta, en lugar de mirar a las personas hacer el trabajo real. El segundo es escribir un enunciado del problema que ya contiene tu solución, como "los técnicos necesitan una app de voz". El tercero es hacer preguntas "¿cómo podríamos…?" tan amplias que no producen nada, o tan estrechas que llevan a una sola función. Y el cuarto es enumerar supuestos sin priorizarlos, de modo que terminas probando primero los cómodos. Tu mapa es un documento vivo. Actualízalo cada semana a medida que llegue evidencia.

## Scene 8 — Recap and exercise (3:05–3:20)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered. Recap lines: "Empathize in the field", "Define a point of view", "Ask how might we", "Map your riskiest assumptions". End card: "Exercise: problem statement and assumption map".
- Narration EN: To recap: empathize in the real setting, define a sharp point of view, ideate with how-might-we questions and map your assumptions. Your exercise this week turns this into a problem statement and a map of your riskiest assumptions.
- Narración ES: En resumen: empatiza en el entorno real, define un punto de vista preciso, idea con preguntas "¿cómo podríamos…?" y mapea tus supuestos. Tu ejercicio de esta semana convierte todo esto en un enunciado del problema y un mapa de tus supuestos más riesgosos.
