## Goal

Get ready to raise. By the end of this exercise you will have a seed deck, a recorded two-minute pitch and a prioritized list of investors, all grounded in the traction and plans you built over the last seven weeks.

Estimated time: 3 to 4 hours.

## Steps

1. Set the milestone and the ask. Write the milestone your round must reach, a rough cost to get there with a buffer, the amount you will raise and the instrument, for example a post-money SAFE.
2. Model your cap table. Using investment ÷ post-money cap for cap SAFEs that convert at their caps, model your planned SAFE round and a later priced round with an option pool top-up. Record the founders' ownership after each step. Treat this as a planning model, not legal or tax advice.
3. Write your one-liner. Test it on at least two people outside your industry and revise it until they can repeat it back.
4. Build the deck. Use the classic structure from lesson 2 in 10 to 12 slides: title and one-liner, problem, solution, why now, traction, market, business model, competition and insight, team, the ask. Put detail such as unit economics, eval results and the cap table in an appendix.
5. Check your traction slide. Separate paying customers from pilots, show trends with dates and define every metric. Include the first results of your week 7 sales sprint.
6. Write and record the two-minute pitch. Use the seven-part structure from lesson 3. Rehearse out loud, cut until it runs at about 1:50, then record one take on video without reading.
7. Build your investor target list. List at least 30 investors who invest at your stage, in your sector and in your geography. For each, note recent relevant investments, whether they could lead, a warm path in if you have one and a fit score from 1 to 3.
8. Draft your first investor update. Write the update you would send at the end of this month, with metrics, highlights, lowlights and asks.

## Deliverable

Upload to your roadmap task:

- the seed deck as a PDF, with appendix;
- a link to your two-minute pitch video;
- your investor target list as a spreadsheet;
- a one-page summary with your milestone, ask, cap table model and draft investor update.

Label every number as either your own data or an estimate.

## How it will be reviewed

Your mentor will check that:

- a stranger can understand what you do from the one-liner and the first slide;
- the deck follows a clear narrative and the traction slide is honest, dated and defined;
- the ask names an amount, an instrument and a milestone, and the cap table math is correct;
- the pitch video stays under two minutes and explains what you do and why users want it;
- the investor list is specific to your stage and sector, with at least 30 named investors.

This is the final deliverable of the program and feeds directly into Demo Day preparation.
