# Video: Designing your MVP
Duration: ~4:00 · Format: 16:9 1920×1080 · Presenter: Maya Ortiz — calm, mid-30s Latin American founder-turned-investor, navy blazer, neutral studio

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:25)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered. Lower third: "Week 4 · Lesson 3". Title in Space Grotesk, Navy: "Designing your MVP".
- Narration EN: With AI coding tools, you can build a convincing product in days. So why not just build it? Because a working product no longer proves anything about your customers. Steve Blank made this point in 2026. The goal of an MVP was never the product. It was always the learning. Let's design the smallest test that teaches you the most.
- Narración ES: Con herramientas de programación con IA puedes construir un producto convincente en días. Entonces, ¿por qué no construirlo y ya? Porque un producto que funciona ya no demuestra nada sobre tus clientes. Steve Blank lo planteó en 2026. El objetivo de un MVP nunca fue el producto. Siempre fue el aprendizaje. Diseñemos la prueba más pequeña que más te enseñe.

## Scene 2 — Build, measure, learn (0:25–0:50)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A circular loop with three nodes, "Build", "Measure", "Learn", drawn in Navy with Steel arrows. A small clock in the center shrinks from "1 quarter" to "1 week" as the loop speeds up. Credit: "Eric Ries, The Lean Startup".
- Narration EN: Eric Ries defines the MVP as the version of a product that gets you the most validated learning for the least effort. It runs inside a loop: build the smallest thing that tests a hypothesis, measure what real customers do, and learn whether to persevere or change course. The goal is to make each loop as short as possible. Weekly loops beat quarterly ones.
- Narración ES: Eric Ries define el MVP como la versión de un producto que te da el mayor aprendizaje validado con el menor esfuerzo. Vive dentro de un ciclo: construyes lo más pequeño que prueba una hipótesis, mides lo que hacen clientes reales y aprendes si perseverar o cambiar de rumbo. La meta es que cada ciclo sea lo más corto posible. Los ciclos semanales le ganan a los trimestrales.

## Scene 3 — Riskiest assumption first (0:50–1:15)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A grid of assumption cards from earlier weeks. Several flip to a Mist "confirmed" state. One remaining card glows in Steel with the label "riskiest open assumption". Beside it, a list of cheap tests appears: "Landing page", "Pre-sale", "Manual service", "Prototype in a sales call".
- Narration EN: Start from your assumption map. Some assumptions are now confirmed by your interviews. Pick the riskiest one still open, the one that would kill the company if it were wrong. Then find the cheapest test of it. Often that is not software at all: a landing page, a pre-sale, a manual service or a prototype shown in a sales call.
- Narración ES: Parte de tu mapa de supuestos. Algunos ya quedaron confirmados por tus entrevistas. Elige el más riesgoso que siga abierto, el que mataría a la empresa si fuera falso. Luego busca la prueba más barata. Muchas veces no es software: una landing page, una preventa, un servicio manual o un prototipo mostrado en una llamada de ventas.

## Scene 4 — Concierge versus Wizard of Oz (1:15–1:45)
- Type: split
- On screen: Two panels. Left, "Concierge": a person visibly handing over a result to a customer, caption "customer knows a person does it". Right, "Wizard of Oz": a curtain with a simple automated interface in front and a person behind, caption "customer sees automation". A thin Steel line between them labeled "consent and data".
- Narration EN: Two MVP types matter most for AI startups. In a concierge MVP, you deliver the service by hand, and the customer knows it. In a Wizard of Oz, the customer sees what looks automated, but a person works behind the curtain. Both produce labeled data you will need later. But Wizard of Oz carries a responsibility: if people would act differently knowing a human is involved, or share sensitive data, choose concierge or tell them.
- Narración ES: Para startups de IA importan sobre todo dos tipos de MVP. En un MVP concierge, entregas el servicio a mano y el cliente lo sabe. En un Mago de Oz, el cliente ve algo que parece automatizado, pero una persona trabaja detrás de la cortina. Ambos producen datos etiquetados que necesitarás después. Pero el Mago de Oz conlleva una responsabilidad: si la gente actuaría distinto al saber que hay una persona, o si comparte datos sensibles, elige concierge o díselo.

## Scene 5 — Cheap tests and labeled data (1:45–2:20)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Four small cards fan out: "Landing page", "Pre-sale or letter of intent", "Clickable prototype", "Single feature". Then a stream of small chat bubbles flows into a table labeled "Question · Answer · Source · Did it work?", which settles into a stack labeled "first eval set · week 5".
- Narration EN: There are other cheap tests too. A landing page that measures sign-ups or deposits. A pre-sale or a letter of intent. A clickable prototype shown in a sales call. Or a product with a single feature. For AI startups, the human-in-the-loop versions have a bonus: every request and every correct answer becomes labeled data. You learn what users really ask and where mistakes are costly, and that becomes your first evaluation set.
- Narración ES: También hay otras pruebas baratas. Una landing page que mide registros o depósitos. Una preventa o una carta de intención. Un prototipo navegable mostrado en una llamada de ventas. O un producto de una sola función. Para startups de IA, las versiones con personas en el ciclo tienen un beneficio extra: cada pedido y cada respuesta correcta se vuelven datos etiquetados. Aprendes qué preguntan de verdad los usuarios y dónde los errores cuestan caro, y eso se convierte en tu primer conjunto de evaluación.

## Scene 6 — Fieldnote's concierge MVP (2:20–2:55)
- Type: broll
- On screen: Caption: "Fieldnote · concierge MVP over WhatsApp". Overlay: a generic chat interface, not branded, showing a photo bubble and a voice-note bubble, then a reply bubble labeled "Fieldnote team".
- Image prompt (if broll): A young man in his early thirties sits at a small desk late afternoon, a phone propped on a stand showing an incoming photo of an equipment nameplate, an open laptop with a technical diagram and a thick manufacturer binder beside him; a woman in her late thirties leans over his shoulder pointing at the diagram. Documentary photograph, natural light, realistic workspace, no logos or readable text.
- Narration EN: Fieldnote's riskiest open question was behavior: would technicians actually reach for help in the middle of a hard job? So they ran a concierge MVP over WhatsApp. Technicians at the five committed shops sent a nameplate photo and a voice note. Tomás answered by hand from manuals and parts catalogs, with Ana backing him on HVAC. Technicians knew the team was answering personally, and shops gave permission before any photo was saved.
- Narración ES: La pregunta abierta más riesgosa de Fieldnote era de comportamiento: ¿los técnicos realmente pedirían ayuda en medio de un trabajo difícil? Así que corrieron un MVP concierge por WhatsApp. Los técnicos de los cinco talleres comprometidos enviaban una foto de la placa y una nota de voz. Tomás respondía a mano con manuales y catálogos de piezas, con Ana de respaldo en HVAC. Los técnicos sabían que el equipo respondía en persona, y los talleres dieron permiso antes de guardar cualquier foto.

## Scene 7 — The experiment plan (2:55–3:25)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A one-page plan builds in six rows: "Hypothesis", "Test", "Metric", "Success line", "Time box and cost", "Decision". Fieldnote's entries fill in briefly: "2 uses a week", "10 techs · 5 shops · 4 weeks", "6 of 10 techs by week 3 + 2 paid pilots". A small "YC: launch early" tag appears in the corner with credit "Michael Seibel, How to Plan an MVP".
- Narration EN: Then they wrote the plan in six parts. Hypothesis, test, metric, success line, time box and cost, and the decision for each outcome. Their line: six of ten technicians using it twice a week by week three, and two shops agreeing to a paid pilot. This is YC's launch-early advice in practice. Michael Seibel puts it simply: set a short deadline, cut the list hard, and ship to real users.
- Narración ES: Luego escribieron el plan en seis partes. Hipótesis, prueba, métrica, criterio de éxito, plazo y costo, y la decisión para cada resultado. Su criterio: seis de diez técnicos usándolo dos veces por semana para la tercera semana, y dos talleres aceptando un piloto pagado. Este es el consejo de YC de lanzar temprano, en la práctica. Michael Seibel lo dice simple: fija una fecha límite corta, recorta la lista sin piedad y lanza a usuarios reales.
