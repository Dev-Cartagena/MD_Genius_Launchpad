## Goal

Turn last week's evidence into two working documents: a bottom-up market sizing sheet that an investor can rebuild, and an MVP experiment plan that tests your riskiest remaining assumption within four weeks. Launch the experiment before the week ends.

Estimated time: 3 to 4 hours.

## Steps

1. Define the unit. Write your ICP in one line and decide what a customer buys: seats, sites, vehicles, jobs or another unit.
2. Count your beachhead customers. Use public sources or a hand-counted sample you scale with a stated method. Write the source and date next to every number, or label it as an estimate.
3. Set a placeholder price from evidence: current spend on workarounds, comparable tools or what interviewees said about budget.
4. Build the sheet. Calculate SAM for your beachhead, a rough TAM and a three-year SOM, all as annual revenue. Add a sensitivity line with a lower price and a smaller customer count.
5. Map the alternatives. List every alternative your interviewees used, including doing nothing, and count each one. Name your 10x dimension and your secret, and write a positioning statement using Moore's template.
6. Plan the MVP. Pick your riskiest open assumption, choose the cheapest test type, and write the six-part plan: hypothesis, test, metric, success line, time box and cost, and decision.
7. Launch. Invite your first participants, ideally the earlyvangelists from week 3, and start the experiment.

## Deliverable

Upload two items:

- A market sizing sheet, as a spreadsheet or one page, showing the formula, each input with its source or estimate label, SAM, TAM, SOM and the sensitivity line, plus a short note on your beachhead and the next market it opens.
- An MVP experiment plan of one to two pages, including your competitive alternatives with counts, your 10x dimension, your positioning statement, the six-part plan, the names or roles of the first participants, and the date the experiment started.

## How it will be reviewed

- Bottom-up logic: the market size is built from customers, units and price, not from a top-down percentage.
- Traceable inputs: every number has a source and date or a clear estimate label, and units are consistent.
- A focused beachhead with a credible path to a larger market.
- Honest competition: alternatives include doing nothing and manual workarounds, drawn from interviews.
- A real experiment: it tests the riskiest open assumption, has a success line and time box set in advance, handles consent and data with care, and has started.
