# Video: Pricing AI products
Duration: ~3:30 · Format: 16:9 1920×1080 · Presenter: Maya Ortiz — calm, mid-30s Latin American founder-turned-investor, navy blazer, neutral studio

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:25)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered. Lower third: "Week 6 · Pricing AI products". Kinetic text: "Picked in an afternoon. Never revisited."
- Narration EN: Most founders choose their price in an afternoon, choose it too low, and never look at it again. With AI, that's especially costly. Your product may replace real work, and every use has a real cost. Today we'll treat price the way we treat everything else in this program: as a hypothesis you test.
- Narración ES: La mayoría de los fundadores elige su precio en una tarde, lo elige demasiado bajo y nunca lo vuelve a mirar. En IA, eso sale especialmente caro. Tu producto puede reemplazar trabajo real, y cada uso tiene un costo real. Hoy vamos a tratar el precio como tratamos todo en este programa: como una hipótesis que se prueba.

## Scene 2 — The pricing thermometer (0:25–1:00)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A vertical thermometer in Steel. Three markers rise from the bottom: "Cost" (Mist), "Price" (Steel), "Value" (Navy). A bracket between Cost and Price reads "your reason to sell". A bracket between Price and Value reads "their reason to buy". Credit line: "Kevin Hale, Startup Pricing 101 (YC)".
- Narration EN: Kevin Hale of YC describes a pricing thermometer. At the bottom is your cost. At the top is the value the customer perceives. Your price sits between them. The gap below price is your margin, your reason to sell. The gap above is the customer's reason to buy. His most repeated advice: founders charge too little. So quantify value in the customer's own numbers, like hours saved or costs avoided, and use your cost only as the floor.
- Narración ES: Kevin Hale, de YC, describe un termómetro de precios. Abajo está tu costo. Arriba está el valor que percibe el cliente. Tu precio está entre los dos. La distancia por debajo del precio es tu margen, tu razón para vender. La distancia por encima es la razón del cliente para comprar. Su consejo más repetido: los fundadores cobran de menos. Así que cuantifica el valor en los números del cliente, como horas ahorradas o costos evitados, y usa tu costo solo como piso.

## Scene 3 — Four pricing models (1:00–1:35)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Four tiles in a 2×2 grid, each flipping to reveal a pro and a con. "Per seat": "easy to budget" / "flat price, variable cost". "Usage": "tracks your cost" / "users ration it". "Outcome": "aligned with value" / "needs a trusted definition of success". "Hybrid": "predictable + protected margin" / "more to explain". The Hybrid tile gets a thin Navy outline labeled "common as of 2026".
- Narration EN: There are four main models. Per seat is easy to budget, but the price stays flat while your costs vary. Usage pricing tracks your costs, but buyers struggle to budget it, and some users ration a tool when they feel a meter running. Outcome pricing, like charging per resolved support ticket, aligns best with value, but both sides must trust the definition of success. Hybrid combines a base fee with a usage allowance, and as of 2026 it's a common choice.
- Narración ES: Hay cuatro modelos principales. Por usuario es fácil de presupuestar, pero el precio se queda fijo mientras tus costos varían. El precio por uso sigue tus costos, pero a los compradores les cuesta presupuestarlo, y algunos usuarios racionan una herramienta cuando sienten que el medidor corre. El precio por resultado, como cobrar por cada ticket de soporte resuelto, es el que mejor se alinea con el valor, pero ambas partes deben confiar en la definición de éxito. El híbrido combina una tarifa base con un volumen de uso incluido, y a 2026 es una elección común.

## Scene 4 — A good value metric (1:35–1:50)
- Type: split
- On screen: Left, Maya. Right, a five-item checklist ticks in one by one: "Grows with customer value", "Predictable for the buyer", "Explained in one sentence", "Reliably measurable", "Healthy margin for heavy users".
- Narration EN: Whatever you choose, test the unit you charge for. It should grow as the customer gets more value, be predictable, be easy to explain, be measurable, and keep your margin healthy even for your heaviest users.
- Narración ES: Elijas lo que elijas, pon a prueba la unidad por la que cobras. Debe crecer cuando el cliente obtiene más valor, ser predecible, fácil de explicar, medible, y mantener sano tu margen incluso con tus usuarios más intensivos.

## Scene 5 — Testing the hypothesis (1:50–2:10)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A sentence template types out in Space Grotesk on White Smoke: "We believe (buyer) will pay (price) per (value metric) because (value). We'll know if (commitment) by (date)." The word "commitment" pulses in Navy, while "compliments" appears in Mist and is struck through.
- Narration EN: Write your price as a testable sentence: who will pay, how much, per what, and why. Then quote it in real sales conversations. Count commitments, not compliments: a signed order, a paid pilot, an approved budget. If nobody pushes back on price, you're probably too cheap.
- Narración ES: Escribe tu precio como una oración que se pueda probar: quién pagará, cuánto, por qué unidad y por qué razón. Luego cotízalo en conversaciones de venta reales. Cuenta compromisos, no elogios: una orden firmada, un piloto pagado, un presupuesto aprobado. Si nadie se queja del precio, probablemente estás cobrando muy poco.

## Scene 6 — Pilots that convert (2:10–2:45)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A timeline bar labeled "Pilot" in Steel with three pins: "Paid from day one", "Success criteria + baseline agreed", "Decision date". At the end, the bar turns Navy and extends into "Annual contract at pre-agreed price". A faded loop above the timeline labeled "pilot purgatory" fades out.
- Narration EN: Then there's pilot purgatory: months of free trials that never convert. Avoid it by designing the pilot as the first stage of a contract. Make it paid, even modestly. Agree on two or three measurable success criteria and a baseline before you start, ideally using the customer's own data. Set a decision date. And write down the price and term of the full contract if the criteria are met.
- Narración ES: Luego está el purgatorio de pilotos: meses de pruebas gratis que nunca se convierten. Evítalo diseñando el piloto como la primera etapa de un contrato. Que sea pagado, aunque sea poco. Acuerda dos o tres criterios de éxito medibles y una línea base antes de empezar, idealmente con los datos del propio cliente. Fija una fecha de decisión. Y deja por escrito el precio y el plazo del contrato completo si se cumplen los criterios.

## Scene 7 — Fieldnote's pricing test (2:45–3:15)
- Type: broll
- On screen: Lower third: "Case: Fieldnote · per technician vs per job (founders' hypothetical test)". Two chips slide in: "Per technician: 2 of 3 pilots preferred", "Per job: risk of rationing".
- Image prompt (if broll): Photorealistic documentary shot inside a mid-size HVAC contractor's office, an operations manager in his fifties at a desk with a dispatch board softly blurred behind him, talking with a woman founder in her thirties holding a tablet with the screen facing away, service vans visible through a window, natural daylight, diverse people, no logos or text in image.
- Narration EN: Fieldnote tested two models with its three pilot contractors: a monthly fee per technician, and a charge per job. Two of three operations managers preferred per technician, because it matched how they budget. One warned that per job pricing might make technicians skip the assistant on simple calls. So the founders' hypothesis is a per-technician fee with a generous allowance and a fair-use limit. And every pilot now has written success criteria and a decision date.
- Narración ES: Fieldnote probó dos modelos con sus tres contratistas piloto: una tarifa mensual por técnico y un cobro por servicio. Dos de los tres gerentes de operaciones prefirieron el precio por técnico, porque encajaba con su forma de presupuestar. Uno advirtió que cobrar por servicio podría hacer que los técnicos dejaran de usar el asistente en llamadas simples. Así que la hipótesis de los fundadores es una tarifa por técnico con un volumen generoso y un límite de uso razonable. Y cada piloto tiene ahora criterios de éxito por escrito y una fecha de decisión.
