## Goal

Build a one-page metrics dashboard that shows, with honest numbers, whether your product is reaching fit and whether its economics can work, and write a pricing hypothesis you will test with real buyers in the next two to four weeks. Mentors and investors should be able to read both in five minutes.

Estimated time: 3 to 4 hours.

## Steps

1. Choose your primary metric (revenue if you charge, otherwise weekly active users or accounts) and three to five input metrics that drive it.
2. Define your activation event in one sentence and measure the share of new users who reach it and the median time it takes.
3. Run the Sean Ellis survey with qualified users only (for example, used the product at least twice in the last two weeks). Add Superhuman's three follow-up questions. Report the percentage very disappointed with the number of responses, then break it down by at least one segment.
4. Build cohort retention for the core action at your product's natural frequency. If you have too little data, show what you have and state when the next cohort will mature.
5. Build a cost model per user or per account per month: tasks, model and API cost per task, share of tasks with human review and the cost per review, hosting and support. Calculate gross margin at your current or candidate price, and at the heaviest 10% of users.
6. Estimate CAC (including founder time), a range for LTV based on gross profit, and payback. Mark every estimate with its assumptions.
7. Run Paul Graham's default alive question with your current burn, cash and recent revenue growth, and write the answer in one line.
8. Write the value you create for one customer in their numbers, compare at least two pricing models against the five value-metric checks, and write your pricing hypothesis: who pays, how much, per what, why, and what commitment by what date will prove it.
9. Draft the terms of your next pilot: price, two or three success criteria with a baseline, a decision date and the pre-agreed price of the full contract.

## Deliverable

- A one-page metrics dashboard (spreadsheet, doc or slide) covering steps 1 to 7.
- A one-page pricing hypothesis covering steps 8 and 9.

## How it will be reviewed

- There is one clear primary metric, and the input metrics plausibly drive it.
- The PMF score shows its sample size and at least one segment, and survey respondents were qualified by usage.
- Retention is shown by cohort, not as a cumulative total.
- Gross margin includes inference and human review, and heavy users are checked.
- Unit economics show their assumptions, and the default alive answer is stated plainly.
- The pricing hypothesis starts from customer value, names a value metric, and defines evidence as a commitment with a date.
- The pilot terms include payment, measurable criteria, a decision date and a conversion price.
