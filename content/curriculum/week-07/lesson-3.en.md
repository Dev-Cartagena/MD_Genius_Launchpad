## Learning objectives

- Explain why distribution deserves as much founder attention as the product itself.
- Use the Bullseye framework to brainstorm channels, run cheap tests and focus on the one that works.
- Tell a funnel from a growth loop, and describe a loop for your product with the metric that shows it is working.
- Choose a content or community play that fits your buyer and that a small team can sustain.

## Overview

Founders tend to believe that a great product spreads by itself. Occasionally it does. Usually it does not, and in AI the problem is sharper: many teams can build a similar product quickly, so the company that wins is often the one that reaches customers faster and more cheaply. Peter Thiel makes this argument in "Zero to One", based on his Stanford CS183 course: distribution is underrated, and a product with no way to reach its customers is not a business. Paul Graham's essay "Startup = Growth" adds the measure. He writes that a good growth rate during YC is 5 to 7% a week. Whatever your stage, growth is the number that tells you whether your distribution works. Last lesson covered selling by hand. This one covers how to find the channels and loops that keep growth going once founders cannot personally touch every deal.

## The Bullseye framework

In their book "Traction", Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares describe 19 channels a startup can use to get customers, including sales, content marketing, search engine optimization, paid ads, partnerships, events, community building, public relations and others. Their key observation is that founders tend to use only the channels they already know, and so miss the one that would work best for them. The Bullseye framework is a simple process to avoid that.

### The three rings

1. Outer ring, what is possible: brainstorm at least one concrete idea for every channel, even ones that seem unlikely. Be specific. "Ads" is not an idea. "Search ads on the exact error codes technicians type into a search engine" is.
2. Middle ring, what is probable: pick the three or so most promising ideas and run a small, cheap test on each. A test answers three questions: how much does a customer cost through this channel, how many customers can it reach, and are they the right customers?
3. Inner ring, what works: put your focus on the single channel that tested best, and keep optimizing it until it stops improving.

Repeat the process when the inner-ring channel saturates. Most startups get the majority of their early growth from one channel, not five.

### How to design a channel test

Keep each test small enough to finish in one to two weeks and cheap enough that a failure does not hurt. Write the hypothesis and the success threshold before you start: "A 15-minute talk at one association chapter meeting will produce at least five qualified discovery calls." Track the result back to closed revenue where you can, not just clicks or sign-ups. A channel that produces many leads but no customers is a distraction.

## Funnels and loops

### The funnel view

A funnel describes a one-way flow: people become aware, show interest, sign up, activate and pay. Funnels are useful for diagnosis. They show where you lose people. But a funnel has no memory. Every month you have to pour new people in at the top, usually by spending more money or more founder time.

### The loop view

A growth loop is a system where the output of one cycle becomes the input of the next. The Reforge essay "Growth Loops are the New Funnels" popularized this framing. Common loops include:

- Referral or viral loops: a user gets value, invites a colleague, and the colleague becomes a user who invites others.
- Content loops: users or the company create content, the content gets found through search or social media, and new users arrive and create more content.
- Paid loops: revenue from new customers funds more advertising, which brings new customers. This works only when payback is short.
- Sales loops: happy customers become references and introductions, which shorten the next sale.

### Measuring a loop

A loop has two numbers that matter: how many new users each cycle produces per existing user, and how long a cycle takes. A referral loop where each active user brings 0.3 new users a quarter is helpful but does not sustain itself. Doubling that rate, or halving the cycle time, compounds. Pick one loop, instrument each step, and improve the weakest step first.

## Content and community

For B2B AI startups, content and community are often the channels that compound best, because buyers in a niche trust peers and specialists more than ads.

### Content that earns trust

Write or record what only you can say. Founder-led content works when it teaches the buyer something useful, such as how to measure a problem, a benchmark from your own work or a teardown of a common failure. If you publish anything derived from customer data, aggregate it, anonymize it and make sure your contracts allow it. Responsible AI from week 2 applies to marketing too.

### Community and channel partners

Industry associations, user groups, trade events and online forums already gather your buyers. Rather than building a community from scratch, show up where one exists and contribute: give talks, answer questions, share templates. Channel partners, such as distributors, integrators or software platforms your buyers already use, can introduce you to many customers at once. They will only do so if you make them look good to their own customers.

## Case study: Fieldnote

Ana and Tomás ran the Bullseye process in one afternoon. Their outer ring included search ads on equipment error codes, short repair videos for technicians, cold email to operations managers, booths at regional trade shows, a regional HVAC contractors association, introductions from parts distributors, integration listings with field-service-management software and referrals from technicians.

They tested three in the middle ring over two weeks. Ana gave a talk on reducing callbacks at one association chapter meeting and offered a free first-time-fix benchmark. That produced seven discovery calls and two pilots (founders' own data). A distributor counter display produced foot traffic but no qualified buyers. Short videos got views from technicians but almost no contact from managers. The association became their inner-ring channel. They now aim to speak at one chapter meeting a month and to write for the association newsletter.

Their growth loop comes from technicians. Techs who use Fieldnote talk to techs at other contractors, and they change employers fairly often. The founders added a "share this fix" feature that sends a colleague a read-only summary of a diagnosis, with an invitation for their manager to see a demo. They measure the loop as referred demo requests per 100 active technicians per month, and they treat the cycle time from share to demo as the number to cut. The founders also check that shared summaries contain no customer names or addresses, so the loop does not create a privacy problem.

## Common mistakes

- Spreading effort thinly across many channels, so none gets enough attention to work.
- Testing a channel without a success threshold written in advance, then calling any result a success.
- Measuring leads or clicks instead of customers and revenue from each channel.
- Calling a one-time burst of attention, such as a launch post, a growth loop.
- Publishing insights from customer data without permission or proper anonymization.

## Your turn

Take 30 minutes. Write one concrete idea for at least ten channels from the Traction list. Circle the three most promising and write a test for each: the hypothesis, the cost, the time box and the success threshold. Then sketch one growth loop for your product as four or five steps in a circle, and name the metric and cycle time you would measure.

## Key takeaways

- Distribution is as important as product, especially in AI, where products are easier to copy.
- The Bullseye framework moves from many channel ideas to a few cheap tests to one focus channel.
- Funnels diagnose where you lose people. Loops compound because each cycle feeds the next.
- Measure a loop by its output per user and its cycle time, and fix the weakest step first.
- For niche B2B buyers, show up in existing communities and partner channels instead of building from zero.
