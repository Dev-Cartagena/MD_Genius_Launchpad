# MD Genius Launchpad: curriculum v1 (draft for review)

An eight-week program in four stages for AI startups in any industry. It draws on classic
and modern startup playbooks: Y Combinator (Startup School, Paul Graham's essays, the YC
library), the Lean LaunchPad and customer development (Steve Blank, taught at Stanford and
Berkeley), Stanford d.school design thinking, Stanford eCorner, Berkeley SkyDeck and Haas
practice, Lean Startup, Jobs-to-be-Done, The Mom Test, Crossing the Chasm, Traction
(Bullseye) and the product-market-fit work of Sean Ellis and Superhuman.

All lesson text is original. Frameworks are credited to their authors and linked to
public sources; nothing is copied from them.

## Structure

- **4 stages × 2 weeks.** Each week is one platform module containing 3 lessons, 1 quiz
  and 1 weekly exercise. That makes 24 lessons and 8 quizzes, in English and Spanish.
- **Each lesson has:**
  - a lecture to read (about 1,200–1,800 words);
  - one worked example from the running case study;
  - an exercise;
  - links to public resources;
  - a 3–5 minute video script with an AI presenter, branded motion graphics and realistic
    B-roll prompts.
- **Each quiz has** 8 multiple-choice questions with explanations. The pass mark is 70%,
  with 3 attempts.
- **Running case study:** *Fieldnote*, a fictional two-founder startup building an AI
  assistant for field-service technicians (HVAC, electrical, solar). It reappears every
  week, so participants see one company go from idea to Demo Day.

## Stage 1 · Foundation (weeks 1–2)

AI fundamentals, problem definition and team formation.

### Week 1: Founders and the AI opportunity

1. **How great startups begin.** "Make something people want"; good ideas versus tarpit
   ideas; founder–market fit; why now.
2. **Where AI creates value.** AI-native workflows, vertical AI, copilots versus agents;
   where the moats come from (proprietary data, workflow embedding, distribution).
3. **Building the founding team.** Co-founder fit, roles, equity and vesting, decision
   making, a weekly operating cadence.

Exercise: write a one-page founder and idea memo.

### Week 2: Defining the problem

1. **Finding a problem worth solving.** Pain severity, frequency and willingness to pay;
   the "hair on fire" test; Jobs-to-be-Done.
2. **Design thinking for founders.** The Stanford d.school modes: empathize, define,
   ideate; problem statements; how-might-we questions; assumption mapping.
3. **Responsible AI from day one.** Data rights and privacy, bias and failure modes,
   human oversight, an overview of current regulation (EU AI Act, sector rules), building
   trust.

Exercise: write a problem statement and a map of your riskiest assumptions.

## Stage 2 · Validation (weeks 3–4)

Market research, customer discovery and MVP planning.

### Week 3: Customer discovery

1. **Get out of the building.** Customer development and the Lean LaunchPad;
   hypotheses, and tests that can prove them wrong.
2. **Interviews that tell the truth.** The Mom Test principles: past behavior over
   opinions, commitment and advancement, recruiting interviewees.
3. **Turning interviews into evidence.** Synthesis, segments, the ideal customer profile,
   early adopters, deciding when to pivot.

Exercise: 10 customer interviews and a synthesis board.

### Week 4: Market and MVP

1. **Sizing the market investors believe.** Bottom-up TAM, SAM and SOM; beachhead
   markets.
2. **Competition and positioning.** Alternatives, including doing nothing; 10x better;
   "secrets" (Zero to One, from Thiel's Stanford CS183); a positioning statement.
3. **Designing your MVP.** Riskiest-assumption tests; concierge and Wizard-of-Oz MVPs;
   build-measure-learn; YC's "launch early".

Exercise: a market sizing sheet and an MVP experiment plan.

## Stage 3 · Development (weeks 5–6)

Technical build, product development and testing.

### Week 5: Building AI products

1. **AI product architecture.** Build versus buy; foundation models, retrieval
   (RAG), fine-tuning and agents; the cost, latency and quality trade-off.
2. **Data and evaluation.** Ground truth, evals, human-in-the-loop, data flywheels,
   monitoring in production.
3. **Ship fast, learn faster.** "Do things that don't scale"; weekly releases; feedback
   loops; technical debt that is worth taking on.

Exercise: an architecture one-pager and an eval set of at least 50 cases.

### Week 6: Product-market fit and metrics

1. **Measuring product-market fit.** The Sean Ellis 40% test, retention curves,
   Superhuman's PMF engine.
2. **Metrics and unit economics.** Activation, retention, CAC, LTV, AI gross margin and
   inference costs, "default alive".
3. **Pricing AI products.** Value-based pricing; per seat, usage or outcome pricing;
   pilots that turn into paid contracts.

Exercise: a metrics dashboard and a pricing hypothesis.

## Stage 4 · Launch (weeks 7–8)

Go-to-market strategy, investor preparation and Demo Day.

### Week 7: Go-to-market and growth

1. **Choosing your go-to-market motion.** Founder-led sales, product-led growth,
   enterprise; crossing the chasm.
2. **Founder-led sales and your first ten customers.** Pipeline, discovery calls,
   pilots, closing, references.
3. **Distribution and growth loops.** The Bullseye framework (Traction), channels,
   loops versus funnels, content and community.

Exercise: a GTM plan and a 30-day sales sprint.

### Week 8: Fundraising and Demo Day

1. **How startup fundraising works.** Pre-seed and seed rounds, the post-money SAFE,
   dilution, when to raise and how much.
2. **The pitch and the deck.** A narrative arc, the classic seed-deck structure,
   traction framing, the ask.
3. **Demo Day and beyond.** The two-minute pitch, investor meetings, follow-up, closing
   the round, investor updates.

Exercise: a seed deck, a two-minute pitch video and an investor target list.
