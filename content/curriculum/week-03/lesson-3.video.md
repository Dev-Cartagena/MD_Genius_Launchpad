# Video: Turning interviews into evidence
Duration: ~3:30 · Format: 16:9 1920×1080 · Presenter: Maya Ortiz — calm, mid-30s Latin American founder-turned-investor, navy blazer, neutral studio

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:25)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered. Lower third: "Week 3 · Lesson 3". Title in Space Grotesk, Navy: "Turning interviews into evidence".
- Narration EN: You have done your interviews. Now comes the step most founders skip. Ten good conversations can still lead to a wrong conclusion, because memory keeps the vivid story and the friendly person, and quietly drops the rest. Synthesis is how you protect yourself from your own memory. Let's turn notes into evidence.
- Narración ES: Ya hiciste tus entrevistas. Ahora viene el paso que la mayoría se salta. Diez buenas conversaciones pueden llevar igual a una conclusión equivocada, porque la memoria guarda la historia más vívida y a la persona más amable, y deja caer el resto en silencio. La síntesis es cómo te proteges de tu propia memoria. Convirtamos notas en evidencia.

## Scene 2 — Debrief and cards (0:25–0:50)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A notes page in White Smoke breaks apart into small rectangular cards, each with one short line. Each card gets a small colored tag in Mist or Steel: "tech, 60-tech shop", "manager, 150-tech shop". The cards drift onto an empty board.
- Narration EN: Start within the hour. After each interview, write a short debrief using the same template every time: who they were, the key facts, their exact words, their workarounds and any commitment. Then break the notes apart. One observation per card. Tag each card with the person's segment. Small cards make patterns visible. Long paragraphs hide them.
- Narración ES: Empieza en la primera hora. Después de cada entrevista, escribe un resumen breve con la misma plantilla cada vez: quién era, los hechos clave, sus palabras exactas, sus soluciones improvisadas y cualquier compromiso. Luego divide las notas. Una observación por tarjeta. Etiqueta cada tarjeta con el segmento de la persona. Las tarjetas pequeñas hacen visibles los patrones. Los párrafos largos los esconden.

## Scene 3 — Cluster and grade (0:50–1:20)
- Type: motion
- On screen: The cards on the board slide into three clusters. Each cluster receives a statement title in Navy: "Techs leave sites without the right part", "Repeat visits are the cost managers feel", "Paperwork is annoying but partly solved". A three-step scale appears at right: "Strong: behavior or commitment", "Medium: specific past story", "Weak: opinion or compliment". Small counters tick up beside each cluster.
- Narration EN: Now cluster. Move similar cards together without deciding the categories first, then name each cluster as a statement, not a topic. "Techs leave sites without the right part" says more than "parts". Then grade the evidence. Strong is behavior you saw or a commitment. Medium is a specific past story. Weak is opinion. Finally, count: how many people, in which segments, at what strength.
- Narración ES: Ahora agrupa. Junta las tarjetas parecidas sin decidir antes las categorías y luego nombra cada grupo como una afirmación, no como un tema. "Los técnicos se van sin la pieza correcta" dice más que "piezas". Después califica la evidencia. Fuerte es un comportamiento que viste o un compromiso. Media es una historia pasada específica. Débil es una opinión. Por último, cuenta: cuántas personas, de qué segmentos y con qué solidez.

## Scene 4 — ICP and earlyvangelists (1:20–1:50)
- Type: split
- On screen: Left, Maya. Right, an ICP card builds line by line: "Company", "Trigger", "Workaround", "People", "Disqualifiers". Below it, a short list headed "Earlyvangelists · Steve Blank" with five ticks: "Has the problem", "Knows it", "Is looking", "Built a workaround", "Has budget". The fourth tick glows briefly in Steel.
- Narration EN: Counted by segment, the differences jump out. Your first market is where the pain is sharpest and the path to purchase is shortest. Write that down as an ideal customer profile, including who is not a fit. Then find your earlyvangelists, Steve Blank's term for the best early adopters. The telling trait is that they have already built a workaround of their own. They are your first test users.
- Narración ES: Al contar por segmento, las diferencias saltan a la vista. Tu primer mercado es donde el dolor es más agudo y el camino a la compra es más corto. Escríbelo como un perfil de cliente ideal, incluido quién no encaja. Luego encuentra a tus earlyvangelists, el término de Steve Blank para los mejores primeros adoptantes. El rasgo que los delata es que ya armaron su propia solución improvisada. Son tus primeras personas usuarias de prueba.

## Scene 5 — Fieldnote's board (1:50–2:25)
- Type: broll
- On screen: Caption: "Fieldnote · 18 interviews · founders' own data". Overlay at the end: three bars labeled "Parts: 8 of 10 techs", "Repeat visits: 6 of 8 managers", "Paperwork: 3 of 10 techs", the last bar marked "below fail line".
- Image prompt (if broll): Two founders stand in front of a whiteboard wall covered in small sticky notes grouped into three clusters, night-time office lit by warm desk lamps, one woman in her late thirties pointing at a cluster, one man in his early thirties holding a marker and a laptop. Documentary photograph, natural shadows, no readable text on the notes, no logos.
- Narration EN: Fieldnote's founders spoke with 18 people. The board was clear. Eight of ten technicians had left a site in the last month unable to identify a part. Six of eight managers and owners named repeat visits as a top-three cost. But only three of ten technicians had lost an hour to paperwork that week. That was below their fail line. Their week 2 estimate needed revising: the lost time was real, but it sat in diagnosis and parts, not reports.
- Narración ES: El equipo de Fieldnote habló con 18 personas. El tablero fue claro. Ocho de diez técnicos se habían ido de un sitio el último mes sin poder identificar una pieza. Seis de ocho gerentes y dueños nombraron las visitas repetidas entre sus tres costos principales. Pero solo tres de diez técnicos habían perdido una hora en papeleo esa semana. Eso quedó por debajo de su criterio de fracaso. Su estimación de la semana 2 necesitaba revisión: el tiempo perdido era real, pero estaba en el diagnóstico y las piezas, no en los reportes.

## Scene 6 — Persevere, adjust or pivot (2:25–2:50)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Three doors in a row labeled "Persevere", "Adjust", "Pivot". Under each, a one-line rule. The middle door opens in Steel. A small inset contrasts "Pivot: one change, backed by evidence" with "Flailing: many random changes", credit "after YC partners".
- Narration EN: So they adjusted. Same customer, new lead problem: first-time fix and parts lookup, at contractors with 20 to 200 technicians. Eric Ries describes a pivot as a structured change in one part of the strategy. YC partners add a warning: one deliberate change backed by evidence is a pivot. A string of random changes is flailing.
- Narración ES: Así que ajustaron. El mismo cliente, un nuevo problema principal: la reparación en la primera visita y la búsqueda de piezas, en contratistas con 20 a 200 técnicos. Eric Ries describe el pivote como un cambio estructurado en una parte de la estrategia. Los socios de YC agregan una advertencia: un cambio deliberado respaldado por evidencia es un pivote. Una serie de cambios al azar es dar tumbos.

## Scene 7 — Fieldnote's new ICP (2:50–3:20)
- Type: broll

- Image prompt (if broll): A service manager in her forties of East Asian descent stands at a dispatch desk in a mid-size HVAC contractor's office, looking at a phone showing a photo sent by a technician, a wall calendar and parts shelves softly out of focus behind her, morning daylight. Documentary photograph, natural color, candid, no logos or readable text.
- Narration EN: Their new ideal customer profile came straight from the board. HVAC contractors with 20 to 200 technicians. A service manager who tracks callbacks. Too few senior technicians to cover every hard call. And one disqualifier: shops where the owner still handles every difficult diagnosis. Their earlyvangelists were obvious, the managers already running night-time photo chats with their senior techs. Those five shops would become the first MVP test in week 4.
- Narración ES: Su nuevo perfil de cliente ideal salió directo del tablero. Contratistas de HVAC con 20 a 200 técnicos. Una gerencia de servicio que registra las visitas repetidas. Muy pocos técnicos senior para cubrir cada llamada difícil. Y un descalificador: talleres donde el dueño todavía resuelve cada diagnóstico complicado. Sus earlyvangelists eran evidentes, los gerentes que ya tenían chats nocturnos de fotos con sus técnicos senior. Esos cinco talleres serían la primera prueba del MVP en la semana 4.

## Scene 8 — Recap and exercise cue (3:20–3:35)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered. Recap line: "Cards. Clusters. Counts. Decision." Steel card: "This week's exercise: 10 interviews and a synthesis board."
- Narration EN: Write it down, cluster it, count it, then decide. This week's exercise brings it together: run at least ten interviews, build your synthesis board, write your ideal customer profile and record your decision with the evidence behind it.
- Narración ES: Escríbelo, agrúpalo, cuéntalo y luego decide. El ejercicio de esta semana lo integra todo: haz al menos diez entrevistas, construye tu tablero de síntesis, escribe tu perfil de cliente ideal y registra tu decisión con la evidencia que la respalda.
