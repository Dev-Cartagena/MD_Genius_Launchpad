## Goal

Replace your riskiest assumptions with evidence from real customers. By the end of the week you will have run at least 10 customer interviews, turned them into a synthesis board, and made a clear decision to persevere, adjust or pivot.

Estimated time: about 3 to 4 hours of preparation and synthesis, plus the interviews themselves, scheduled across the week (about 20 to 30 minutes each).

## Steps

1. Write your hypotheses. Take the three riskiest assumptions from your week 2 map and rewrite each one with a who, a what, a method and a fail line. Set the fail lines before the first interview.
2. Write your interview guide. Use the Mom Test rules: an opening that does not mention your product, five questions about specific past events, two follow-up probes and one commitment ask that fits your stage.
3. Recruit. List at least 15 people who have the problem now, across the roles involved: the person who feels the pain, the person who decides and the person who pays. Use warm introductions first, and ask every interviewee for two more names.
4. Run at least 10 interviews. One founder asks, one takes notes. Record only with permission. Write a debrief within the hour after each one, using the same template every time.
5. Build the synthesis board. Write one observation per card, tag each card by segment, cluster the cards, name each cluster as a statement, and count and grade the evidence behind it as strong, medium or weak.
6. Decide. Compare each result with its fail line and mark it confirmed, rejected or unclear. Write your ideal customer profile, name your earlyvangelists and record your decision: persevere, adjust or pivot.

## Deliverable

Upload one document or link, two to four pages, containing:

- Your hypotheses with their fail lines, and the result for each.
- An interview log: role, company size and segment, date and the commitment you asked for and received. Remove personal names if your interviewees prefer.
- A photo or export of your synthesis board, with your top three clusters, their counts and evidence strength.
- Your ideal customer profile, including at least one disqualifier.
- A list of your earlyvangelists and the workaround each one already uses.
- Your decision, in one paragraph, with the evidence behind it.

## How it will be reviewed

- Volume and fit: at least 10 interviews with people who have the problem, across more than one role.
- Honest method: questions about past behavior, fail lines set in advance, and compliments not counted as evidence.
- Quality of synthesis: findings are counted by segment and graded by strength, not summarized from memory.
- A clear decision that follows from the evidence, with an ICP that traces back to the board.
- Commitments: at least two concrete next steps from prospects, such as a trial, an introduction or a deposit.
