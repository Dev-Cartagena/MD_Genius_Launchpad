Duration: ~3:30 · Format: 16:9 1920×1080 · Presenter: Maya Ortiz — calm, mid-30s Latin American founder-turned-investor, navy blazer, neutral studio

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:25)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered, medium shot. Lower third in Space Grotesk, Navy #082B42 bar with White Smoke text: "Week 3 · Customer discovery". After 4 seconds a single line fades in at right in Steel #3A607D: "Get out of the building".
- Narration EN: With AI tools, you can build a product that looks finished in a weekend. That sounds like good news, and it is. But it also means a working product proves very little. The hard part is no longer building. The hard part is knowing what to build, and for whom. This week, you find that out the only way it can be found: by talking to customers.
- Narración ES: Con herramientas de IA puedes construir en un fin de semana un producto que parece terminado. Suena a buena noticia, y lo es. Pero también significa que un producto que funciona demuestra muy poco. Lo difícil ya no es construir. Lo difícil es saber qué construir y para quién. Esta semana lo descubres de la única forma posible: hablando con clientes.

## Scene 2 — A startup is a search (0:25–0:50)
- Type: motion
- On screen: White Smoke background. The sentence "A startup is an organization formed to search for a repeatable and scalable business model" types on in Navy. The words "search", "repeatable" and "scalable" lift out one by one in Steel, each with a two-word caption below: "no answer yet", "win the next one the same way", "each customer costs less". Small credit at bottom left: "Steve Blank".
- Narration EN: Steve Blank, who created the Lean LaunchPad at Stanford, defines a startup as an organization formed to search for a repeatable and scalable business model. Search means you do not know the answer yet. Repeatable means you can win the next customer the way you won the last. Scalable means each new customer costs less than they bring in. A big company executes. A startup searches.
- Narración ES: Steve Blank, creador del Lean LaunchPad en Stanford, define una startup como una organización creada para buscar un modelo de negocio repetible y escalable. Buscar significa que aún no conoces la respuesta. Repetible significa que puedes ganar al siguiente cliente como ganaste al anterior. Escalable significa que cada cliente nuevo cuesta menos de lo que aporta. Una gran empresa ejecuta. Una startup busca.

## Scene 3 — The four steps (0:50–1:15)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Four rounded blocks in a row, labeled "Customer discovery", "Customer validation", "Customer creation", "Company building". The first block is Navy and the rest Mist #97B7C1. A curved arrow loops from block two back to block one, labeled "pivot". A thin bracket over block one reads "Weeks 3–4".
- Narration EN: Blank's customer development has four steps. Discovery: test whether the problem is real and whether your solution matters. Validation: test whether you can sell it again and again. Then creation, where you spend to grow demand, and company building. If discovery fails, you loop back and change a hypothesis. That loop is a pivot. This week and next, you live in step one.
- Narración ES: El desarrollo de clientes de Blank tiene cuatro pasos. Descubrimiento: probar si el problema es real y si tu solución importa. Validación: probar si puedes venderla una y otra vez. Luego la creación, donde inviertes para generar demanda, y la construcción de la empresa. Si el descubrimiento falla, regresas y cambias una hipótesis. Ese ciclo es un pivote. Esta semana y la próxima vives en el primer paso.

## Scene 4 — Hypotheses you can break (1:15–1:45)
- Type: split
- On screen: Left half, Maya speaking. Right half, two cards stacked. Top card labeled "Weak" in Mist: "Technicians want AI help." It fades to 40 percent opacity. Bottom card labeled "Strong" in Navy, with four tags appearing in sequence on its edge: "Who", "What", "How we check", "Fail line". Card text: "6 of 10 technicians will describe a job last month they left unfixed because they could not identify a part. Fail line: fewer than 4."
- Narration EN: Most founders write hypotheses that cannot fail. "Technicians want AI help" is impossible to disprove, so it teaches you nothing. A useful hypothesis names who, what, how you will check it, and the result that would make you give it up. Write that fail line before your first conversation. If you set it afterwards, you will set it wherever the data happens to land. That one habit separates evidence from storytelling.
- Narración ES: La mayoría de las personas fundadoras escribe hipótesis que no pueden fallar. "Los técnicos quieren ayuda de IA" es imposible de refutar, así que no te enseña nada. Una hipótesis útil nombra quién, qué, cómo lo comprobarás y el resultado que te haría abandonarla. Escribe ese criterio de fracaso antes de tu primera conversación. Si lo defines después, lo pondrás justo donde caigan los datos. Ese solo hábito separa la evidencia de la narrativa.

## Scene 7 — Who to talk to (2:40–3:05)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Three overlapping circles in Mist, Steel and Navy, labeled "Feels the pain", "Decides", "Pays". A small icon row below shows sources appearing one by one: "Your network", "Associations", "Forums", "Suppliers", "LinkedIn by job title". A final arrow labeled "Ask for two more names" loops from the last source back to the first.
- Narration EN: Who should you talk to? People who have the problem now, not people who might have it someday. In business software, the person who feels the pain, the person who decides and the person who pays are often different people. You need all three views. Start with warm introductions, then widen: associations, forums, suppliers, job-title searches. And at the end of every conversation, ask for two more names.
- Narración ES: ¿Con quién hablar? Con personas que tienen el problema hoy, no con quienes podrían tenerlo algún día. En el software para empresas, quien siente el dolor, quien decide y quien paga suelen ser personas distintas. Necesitas las tres miradas. Empieza con presentaciones cálidas y luego amplía: asociaciones, foros, proveedores, búsquedas por cargo. Y al final de cada conversación, pide dos nombres más.
