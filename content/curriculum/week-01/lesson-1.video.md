## Scene 2 — Make something people want (0:20–0:50)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Kinetic text on Navy: "Make something people want" fades in word by word, credited "Y Combinator" in Mist below. Then a split diagram in Steel #3A607D: on the left a wide, shallow bowl labeled "many people, mild want"; on the right a narrow, deep well labeled "few people, urgent want". - Narration EN: Y Combinator's motto is simple: make something people want. Real wanting shows up as behavior. People are already hacking together a fix with spreadsheets, phone calls or paid help. Paul Graham describes the ideal early market as a narrow, deep well, a small group that needs your product urgently, rather than a crowd that likes it a little.
- Narración ES: El lema de Y Combinator es simple: haz algo que la gente quiera. El deseo real se nota en el comportamiento. La gente ya está armando una solución con hojas de cálculo, llamadas o ayuda pagada. Paul Graham describe el mercado inicial ideal como un pozo estrecho y profundo: un grupo pequeño que necesita tu producto con urgencia, y no una multitud a la que le gusta un poco.

## Scene 3 — Tarpit ideas (0:50–1:20)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A dark Steel pit labeled "Tarpit idea". Three idea cards slide toward it and sink slowly: "Plans with friends", "Recommend restaurants", "A chatbot for everything". A checklist appears beside the pit in White Smoke: "Mild problem", "Needs mass habit change", "No buyer with a budget", "Found by brainstorming". Each line ticks on in sequence.
- Narration EN: YC partners Dalton Caldwell and Michael Seibel call some ideas tarpits. They look easy, many smart teams have tried them, and they keep failing for structural reasons. The problem is mild, nobody has a budget for it, or it needs millions of people to change their habits at once. Generic AI chatbots are this decade's version. They demo well and retain poorly.
- Narración ES: Dalton Caldwell y Michael Seibel, socios de YC, llaman a algunas ideas "tarpits", o ideas trampa. Parecen fáciles, muchos equipos inteligentes las han intentado y siguen fracasando por razones estructurales. El problema es leve, nadie tiene presupuesto para resolverlo o exige que millones de personas cambien sus hábitos al mismo tiempo. Los chatbots genéricos de IA son la versión de esta década: lucen bien en una demo y retienen mal.

## Scene 4 — Founder–market fit (1:20–1:45)
- Type: split
- On screen: Left half, Maya speaking. Right half, three stacked bars that fill in Steel with Mist labels: "Insight", "Access", "Staying power". Below them a small caption: "Can be earned. Cannot be skipped."
- Narration EN: Great ideas are usually noticed, not invented, by people close to a problem. That is founder–market fit. Ask yourself three questions. Do you understand this problem better than outsiders? Can you reach twenty potential customers this month without ads? And will you still care in year five, when the work is tedious? If the honest answer is no, earn it before you build.
- Narración ES: Las grandes ideas normalmente se notan, no se inventan, y las notan personas cercanas al problema. Eso es el encaje fundador–mercado. Hazte tres preguntas. ¿Entiendes este problema mejor que la gente de afuera? ¿Puedes hablar con veinte clientes potenciales este mes sin publicidad? ¿Te seguirá importando en el quinto año, cuando el trabajo sea tedioso? Si la respuesta honesta es no, gánatelo antes de construir.

## Scene 5 — Why now (1:45–2:10)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A horizontal timeline in Mist. A marker labeled "Before" shows a crossed-out microphone icon over a noise waveform. The marker slides to "Now" and the waveform resolves into clean text. Two short lines appear: "Weak: AI is getting better." (in Steel, struck through) and "Strong: a specific change, a specific user." (in White Smoke).
- Narration EN: Investors always ask: why now? A weak answer is "AI is getting better." A strong answer names one specific change and the user it unlocks. Speech models that work on noisy job sites, plus a phone in every worker's pocket, is a why now. Write yours in two sentences.
- Narración ES: Los inversionistas siempre preguntan: ¿por qué ahora? Una respuesta débil es "la IA está mejorando". Una respuesta fuerte nombra un cambio concreto y el usuario al que le abre una posibilidad. Modelos de voz que funcionan en obras ruidosas, más un teléfono en el bolsillo de cada trabajador, es un "por qué ahora". Escribe el tuyo en dos oraciones.

## Scene 6 — Fieldnote on the rooftop (2:10–2:45)
- Type: broll
- On screen: Slow push-in on the image. Lower third: "Case study · Fieldnote". A caption fades in near the end: "Ana · operations · Tomás · ML engineering".
- Image prompt (if broll): Photorealistic documentary photo of a Latina HVAC technician in her early thirties on a commercial rooftop at late afternoon, kneeling beside an open air-conditioning unit, holding a smartphone to her ear while looking at a panel of wiring, tools on the gravel roof, natural warm light, shallow depth of field, candid, no logos or readable text.
- Narration EN: Meet our case study, Fieldnote. Ana spent close to a decade in HVAC operations. Every week she watched technicians get stuck on a fault code, call a colleague, guess at a part and write reports late at night. Tomás, a machine learning engineer, rode along and saw that technicians' hands are always busy. So they are building a voice assistant for field technicians. Ana brings insight and access. Tomás brings the technology. And speech models are finally good enough.
- Narración ES: Te presentamos nuestro caso, Fieldnote. Ana pasó cerca de una década en operaciones de HVAC. Cada semana veía a técnicos atascarse con un código de falla, llamar a un colega, adivinar un repuesto y escribir reportes de noche. Tomás, ingeniero de machine learning, los acompañó en sus rutas y vio que los técnicos siempre tienen las manos ocupadas. Por eso construyen un asistente de voz para técnicos de campo. Ana aporta conocimiento y acceso. Tomás aporta la tecnología. Y los modelos de voz por fin son lo bastante buenos.

## Scene 7 — Test your idea (2:45–3:25)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Four numbered question cards stack on Navy #082B42, each sliding in from the right in White Smoke: "1. Who exactly has this problem?", "2. What do they do today, and what does it cost?", "3. How would you know within weeks?", "4. Would a handful use a rough version every week?". Cards 1 and 2 get a Mist #97B7C1 outline and the label "Can't answer these? You have a topic, not an idea."
- Narration EN: Before you build, put your idea through four questions. Who exactly has this problem? Name a role, a company type or a situation. What do they do about it today, and what does that cost them in time, money or risk? How would you know, within a few weeks, that they want your solution? And would at least a handful of people use a rough first version every week? If you cannot answer the first two concretely, you do not have an idea yet. You have a topic. That is fine on day one. It is not fine on day thirty.
- Narración ES: Antes de construir, pasa tu idea por cuatro preguntas. ¿Quién tiene exactamente este problema? Nombra un cargo, un tipo de empresa o una situación. ¿Qué hace hoy para resolverlo y cuánto le cuesta en tiempo, dinero o riesgo? ¿Cómo sabrías, en pocas semanas, que quiere tu solución? ¿Y al menos unas cuantas personas usarían cada semana una primera versión imperfecta? Si no puedes responder las dos primeras con precisión, todavía no tienes una idea. Tienes un tema. Eso está bien el primer día. No lo está el día treinta.
