# Video: Building the founding team
Duration: ~4:00 · Format: 16:9 1920×1080 · Presenter: Maya Ortiz — calm, mid-30s Latin American founder-turned-investor, navy blazer, neutral studio

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:20)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered. Lower third: "Week 1 · Building the founding team". Two small circles in Mist #97B7C1 drift toward each other behind the title and link with a thin line.
- Narration EN: At the earliest stage, investors back teams before they back products, because the product will change many times. And many startups do not die from competition. They die from problems between the founders. Most of those problems are predictable. Let's prevent them.
- Narración ES: En la etapa más temprana, los inversionistas apuestan por equipos antes que por productos, porque el producto va a cambiar muchas veces. Y muchas startups no mueren por la competencia. Mueren por problemas entre los fundadores. La mayoría de esos problemas son predecibles. Vamos a prevenirlos.

## Scene 2 — Co-founder fit (0:20–0:50)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Four cards flip over one by one in Steel #3A607D with White Smoke text: "Shared values and ambition", "Complementary skills", "Trust under stress", "Equal commitment". Then a short three-step track animates below: "Real project, 2–4 weeks" then "Hard conversations" then "Reference checks".
- Narration EN: A co-founder is the person you will make your hardest decisions with, for years. Look for shared values and ambition, complementary skills, trust under stress and equal commitment. The best evidence is having worked together before. If you have not, create that history: ship a real project together for a few weeks, have the hard conversations about money, time and who leads, and check references like you would for a senior hire.
- Narración ES: Un cofundador es la persona con la que tomarás tus decisiones más difíciles, durante años. Busca valores y ambición compartidos, habilidades complementarias, confianza bajo presión y un compromiso equivalente. La mejor evidencia es haber trabajado juntos antes. Si no lo han hecho, construyan esa historia: entreguen un proyecto real juntos durante unas semanas, tengan las conversaciones difíciles sobre dinero, dedicación y liderazgo, y pidan referencias como lo harías para una contratación senior.

## Scene 3 — Roles and decisions (0:50–1:20)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A grid of area tiles: "Product", "Engineering", "Customers", "Sales", "Finance", "Legal", "Hiring". Each tile gets one colored owner dot, Navy for founder A and Steel for founder B. Then a fork icon splits into two lanes: "Reversible: owner decides fast" and "Hard to reverse: written proposal".
- Narration EN: Give every area one owner, including the boring ones like finance and legal. Owners decide quickly, with input. For the few shared decisions, agree in advance how you will decide. Many teams name one founder CEO with the final say after real discussion, to avoid deadlock. And separate reversible decisions, made fast, from hard-to-reverse ones, which deserve a written proposal.
- Narración ES: Asigna un responsable a cada área, incluidas las aburridas como finanzas y temas legales. Los responsables deciden rápido, escuchando opiniones. Para las pocas decisiones compartidas, acuerden de antemano cómo van a decidir. Muchos equipos nombran a un fundador como CEO, con la última palabra después de una discusión real, para evitar bloqueos. Y separen las decisiones reversibles, que se toman rápido, de las difíciles de revertir, que merecen una propuesta escrita.

## Scene 4 — Equity and vesting (1:20–2:05)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A pie chart in Mist and Steel splits into two equal halves labeled "Founder A" and "Founder B". Next, a four-year timeline: year one stays empty with a small wall icon labeled "1-year cliff", then a block of 25% fills at the one-year mark, and the remaining 75% fills in small monthly steps to year four. Caption: "Common US structure. Confirm with a startup lawyer."
- Narration EN: On equity, Michael Seibel of YC argues co-founders should usually split equally, because building a company takes many years and the first few months should not decide ownership for a decade. Then add vesting. The most common US structure is four years with a one-year cliff. Nothing vests in year one, a quarter vests at the one-year mark, and the rest vests monthly. Vesting protects the founder who stays, and it makes the company fundable. Do the paperwork early with a lawyer, including IP assignment to the company.
- Narración ES: Sobre la participación, Michael Seibel, de YC, sostiene que los cofundadores normalmente deberían repartirla en partes iguales, porque construir una empresa toma muchos años y los primeros meses no deberían definir la propiedad de una década. Luego agrega vesting. La estructura más común en Estados Unidos es de cuatro años con un cliff de un año. Nada se consolida en el primer año, una cuarta parte se consolida al cumplirlo y el resto mes a mes. El vesting protege al fundador que se queda y hace que la empresa sea financiable. Haz los trámites pronto con un abogado, incluida la cesión de propiedad intelectual a la empresa.

## Scene 5 — Weekly cadence (2:05–2:25)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A week strip Monday to Friday. Monday lights up: "Planning · top 3 goals, one owner each". Each day shows a small dot: "Daily check-in". Midweek: "Metrics review". Friday: "Retro". A sample goal morphs from "Talk to customers" (struck through in Steel) to "8 discovery calls" (White Smoke).
- Narration EN: Finally, a rhythm. Plan on Monday: three goals, each with one owner and a checkable result. Check in briefly every day. Review a few metrics weekly. Hold a short retro on Friday and ask one honest question: is there anything we are not saying to each other? Write goals as outcomes, not activities.
- Narración ES: Por último, un ritmo. Planifiquen el lunes: tres metas, cada una con un responsable y un resultado verificable. Hagan un check-in breve cada día. Revisen pocas métricas cada semana. Hagan una retrospectiva corta el viernes y una pregunta honesta: ¿hay algo que no nos estemos diciendo? Escriban las metas como resultados, no como actividades.

## Scene 7 — Paperwork and common mistakes (3:00–3:40)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A checklist in White Smoke on Navy #082B42 ticks on line by line: "Legal entity", "Founder stock with vesting", "IP assigned to the company", "83(b) election in the US, generally within 30 days". A small caption: "As of 2026. Rules vary by country. Work with a startup lawyer." Then three warning tags in Steel #3A607D slide in: "Co-founder by convenience", "Delayed equity talk", "No vesting".
- Narration EN: A few pieces of paperwork are worth doing early, with a startup lawyer. Form a proper legal entity. Sign founder stock agreements with vesting. Assign all intellectual property, the code, the models and the data, to the company. And in the US, ask about the 83(b) tax election on day one, because the filing window is short, generally thirty days. The most common team mistakes are choosing a co-founder because they are available, delaying the equity conversation because it feels awkward, and skipping vesting. Each one gets harder to fix every month you wait.
- Narración ES: Hay algunos trámites que conviene hacer pronto, con un abogado especializado en startups. Constituye una entidad legal adecuada. Firma contratos de acciones de fundadores con vesting. Cede a la empresa toda la propiedad intelectual: el código, los modelos y los datos. Y en Estados Unidos, pregunta por la elección fiscal 83(b) el primer día, porque el plazo es corto, en general treinta días. Los errores de equipo más comunes son elegir cofundador porque está disponible, postergar la conversación sobre participación porque es incómoda y omitir el vesting. Cada uno se vuelve más difícil de corregir con cada mes que esperas.
