# Video: Ship fast, learn faster
Duration: ~3:15 · Format: 16:9 1920×1080 · Presenter: Maya Ortiz — calm, mid-30s Latin American founder-turned-investor, navy blazer, neutral studio

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:20)
- Type: presenter
- On screen: Maya centered. Lower third: "Week 5 · Ship fast, learn faster". Kinetic text at right in Navy: "Months of building. Weeks of learning."
- Narration EN: Here's a quiet way startups die. They build for months, polish everything, and then learn in a week that they built the wrong thing. Messy code is not your biggest risk right now. Learning too slowly is. Today is about learning faster without breaking trust.
- Narración ES: Hay una forma silenciosa en que mueren las startups. Construyen durante meses, pulen todo y luego descubren en una semana que construyeron lo equivocado. El código desordenado no es tu mayor riesgo ahora. Aprender demasiado lento sí. Hoy hablamos de aprender más rápido sin romper la confianza.

## Scene 2 — Do things that don't scale (0:20–0:50)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Title card "Do Things That Don't Scale · Paul Graham, 2013" in Space Grotesk. Three icons animate in a row in Steel: a handshake "Recruit by hand", a heart outline "Absurd attention", a gear with a hand "Do the work behind the product". A dotted line runs beneath them labeled "learn what to automate".
- Narration EN: Paul Graham's essay "Do Things That Don't Scale" makes a simple point. The work that gets a startup off the ground looks inefficient, so founders avoid it. Recruit your first users by hand. Stripe's founders would ask for your laptop and set you up on the spot. Give early users an absurd level of attention. And in AI products, do part of the work behind the product yourself.
- Narración ES: El ensayo de Paul Graham "Do Things That Don't Scale" plantea algo simple. El trabajo que despega a una startup parece ineficiente, y por eso los fundadores lo evitan. Consigue a tus primeros usuarios a mano. Los fundadores de Stripe te pedían la laptop y te dejaban todo configurado ahí mismo. Dales a los primeros usuarios una atención desproporcionada. Y en productos de IA, haz tú parte del trabajo detrás del producto.

## Scene 3 — Manual work as a learning tool (0:50–1:10)
- Type: split
- On screen: Left, Maya. Right, a simple gauge labeled "founder hours per customer" in Mist, with a threshold line in Navy labeled "automate when you understand it".
- Narration EN: Load customer documents yourself. Review uncertain answers by hand. Each manual step shows you what good looks like before you automate it. But manual work is a learning tool, not a business model. Track the hours, and automate once you understand the step.
- Narración ES: Carga tú los documentos de los clientes. Revisa a mano las respuestas dudosas. Cada paso manual te muestra cómo se ve un buen resultado antes de automatizarlo. Pero el trabajo manual es una herramienta para aprender, no un modelo de negocio. Mide las horas y automatiza cuando ya entiendas el paso.

## Scene 4 — The weekly release rhythm (1:10–1:45)
- Type: motion
- On screen: A weekly calendar strip in White Smoke with one day highlighted in Navy, "Release day". A conveyor of small boxes moves toward it and passes through a gate labeled "Eval set" in Steel. One box with a Steel warning outline stops at the gate with the caption "category got worse: hold". The others pass and become a short card "Release notes to users".
- Narration EN: YC has always said: launch early, then keep launching. Inside your product, that means a weekly release day. It forces you to cut work into pieces you can finish. But in AI, speed is only safe if you know what each change did. So every release passes through your eval set first. If a category gets worse, the change waits. Then tell users what changed, and who asked for it. Nothing brings in more feedback than showing people theirs was used.
- Narración ES: YC siempre lo ha dicho: lanza temprano y sigue lanzando. Dentro de tu producto, eso significa un día fijo de lanzamiento cada semana. Te obliga a dividir el trabajo en piezas que puedas terminar. Pero en IA, la velocidad solo es segura si sabes qué hizo cada cambio. Así que cada lanzamiento pasa primero por tu set de evaluación. Si una categoría empeora, el cambio espera. Luego cuéntales a los usuarios qué cambió y quién lo pidió. Nada atrae más feedback que mostrarle a la gente que el suyo se usó.

## Scene 5 — Feedback loops (1:45–2:05)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Three inputs flow into a single board labeled "Weekly triage": "Observation", "Log review", "User requests". The board outputs one arrow labeled "Next release" that loops back to the users.
- Narration EN: Then build loops that bring evidence back. Watch people use the product at work. Read a sample of real logs every week as a team. And run one triage meeting where bugs, eval failures and requests compete on one board.
- Narración ES: Luego crea ciclos que traigan evidencia de vuelta. Observa a la gente usar el producto en su trabajo. Lee en equipo una muestra de registros reales cada semana. Y haz una sola reunión de priorización donde errores, fallas de evaluación y pedidos compitan en un mismo tablero.

## Scene 6 — Debt worth taking (2:05–2:30)
- Type: motion
- On screen: Two columns. Left, "Take it" in Steel: "Manual ops", "Hard-coded settings", "Thin model wrapper", "Simple infrastructure". Right, "Never" in Navy: "Weak security", "Mixed customer data", "No evals", "No logs", "No backups". A small notebook icon below labeled "Debt register: reason + trigger".
- Narration EN: Some technical debt is a smart loan. Manual operations, hard-coded settings, simple infrastructure: they buy learning now. Other debt can sink you. Never cut corners on security, customer data separation, evals, logging or backups. Write every shortcut in a debt register, with the reason and the trigger to pay it back.
- Narración ES: Algunas deudas técnicas son un préstamo inteligente. Operaciones manuales, configuraciones fijas, infraestructura simple: compran aprendizaje ahora. Otras deudas pueden hundirte. Nunca recortes en seguridad, separación de datos de clientes, evaluaciones, registros o copias de seguridad. Anota cada atajo en un registro de deuda, con su motivo y el disparador para pagarlo.

## Scene 7 — Fieldnote on the roof (2:30–3:00)
- Type: broll
- On screen: Lower third: "Case: Fieldnote · ride-alongs and Thursday releases".
- Image prompt (if broll): Photorealistic documentary shot on a commercial rooftop with packaged HVAC units, a technician in a hard hat speaking into a phone while a woman in her thirties with a notebook observes beside him, bright overcast daylight, wind-blown hair, city skyline softly out of focus, diverse people, no logos or text in image.
- Narration EN: Here's Fieldnote. Ana spent full days riding along with technicians at their first pilot contractor. On rooftops and in attics, she saw that noise garbled model numbers. So the team added a step: the assistant reads the identified unit back before it answers. Tomás loads every contractor's manuals by hand and reviews each low-confidence answer himself. Releases ship on Thursdays, but only after the 120-case eval set runs. And each contractor's data always stays separate.
- Narración ES: Así lo vivió Fieldnote. Ana pasó días completos acompañando a técnicos de su primer contratista piloto. En techos y áticos, vio que el ruido distorsionaba los números de modelo. Así que el equipo agregó un paso: el asistente repite el equipo identificado antes de responder. Tomás carga a mano los manuales de cada contratista y revisa él mismo cada respuesta de baja confianza. Los lanzamientos salen los jueves, pero solo después de correr el set de 120 casos. Y los datos de cada contratista siempre se mantienen separados.

- Narration EN: Do it by hand, ship every week, and let your evals be the brakes that let you go fast. Now finish this week's exercise: your architecture one-pager and an eval set of at least fifty cases.
- Narración ES: Hazlo a mano, lanza cada semana y deja que tus evaluaciones sean los frenos que te permiten ir rápido. Ahora termina el ejercicio de esta semana: tu one-pager de arquitectura y un set de evaluación de al menos cincuenta casos.
